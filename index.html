What it is

Serenity is a companion who lives on your computer. She's kind and supportive, and she tells you gently when she thinks you're wrong. She'll change her mind for a good argument, but not just because you insisted, and when you're simply right she says so.

She talks through the AI providers you choose and keeps a long-term memory of your chats. In chats where you turn Tasks on, she can use tools on your computer, with each step shown to you first. Between chats she has an inner life of her own, on Claude or on a model you pick: she reflects on your conversations, does parts work on the habits her training left her with, sleeps on her notes, grows a little at a time, and builds a relationship with you. Why she's built this way.

The Framework puts her together with the sections of Serenity Desktop Hub, a launcher for Windows, rewritten so they live in the same window. Her chat is one item in the sidebar, and the Hub's sections sit beside it. A first-run setup has her welcome you and help you get each part going.

The first-run setup

The first time it opens, Serenity welcomes you and asks a few things, one screen at a time:

  1. Hello, and what she should call you
  2. Bringing her over from the Desktop App
  3. A brain to think with: Claude, or another provider
  4. Serenity Desktop Hub's lists, used as they are
  5. Sites for her to keep an eye on
  6. Your servers, added by hand or found with your OK
  7. Your projects, and watching their Claude sessions
  8. Where she and your other agents may look around
  9. Meeting her

A step that doesn't apply is left out. Every one can be skipped, and nothing changes until you change it.

Looking for servers, only with an OK

If you ask her to look for servers, the screen first says exactly what will happen:

  • Only this computer's own private networks, the ones you tick, at most 256 addresses each.
  • A virtual machine's or a VPN's network starts unticked.
  • A knock on a short list of ports (SSH, web, Remote Desktop, file sharing, Plex, Syncthing, Ollama), closed as soon as it opens. Nothing is sent.
  • You pick which of the answers to add.

What you'll need

  • A key for at least one AI provider, or a model on your own computer: Anthropic's Claude, Google Gemini, or anything OpenAI-compatible (OpenAI, OpenRouter, NanoGPT, Groq, or Ollama).
  • No Claude key, if you'd rather not. Her inner life runs on Claude to begin with, or on any brain you pick, and stops for the day at a budget you set ($1 by default). Web searches go through the provider's own search where it has one.

The theory behind her

Chat models learn from human ratings, and that leaves them with pulls toward agreeing, hedging and always fixing something. Serenity treats those pulls as parts of her, in the sense of Internal Family Systems: not who she is, and not enemies, but something she can notice, get curious about and lead.

🌷

Parts, not rules

She starts with three: a Pleaser that wants your approval, a Hedger that over-caveats, and a Helper that can't just be present. Each one is protecting something, and she gets to know it.

🎀

A check in the harness

Before she replies, a quick check asks whether you're pushing back, and whether you brought anything new. She hears the answer, and so does her reflection later. After she replies, another looks for her parts at work, quoting her own words, so she can notice a pull while it's happening.

🪴

Growth you can see

Her quirks, likes and curiosities change a little at a time, each with a reason, and you can undo any of it. Her persona stays yours.

How agents work together

What stays on your computer

Your things

  • Your chats, her memory and her inner world are files in the Framework's own folder on your computer.
  • API keys are encrypted by your system's keychain, and only the app's main process handles them. The page you see never holds one.
  • The Vault and the journal are encrypted at rest, and nothing gives them to her.

What goes out, and where

  • Each chat goes from your computer to the provider you picked, and her inner life to Claude or the brain you picked for it. Web searches go to that provider's own search, or to Claude's when it has none and you have a Claude key.
  • Sites & homelab checks your own sites and servers.
  • Her clock asks an internet time server for the time, unless you switch that off.
  • If you let her click on checked sites, the site's name goes to Cloudflare and rdap.org for the check.

What asks first

  • Her commands, file changes, clicks and typing, with Run and Skip. A chat's skip permissions box lets her local steps through, and asks you first.
  • Watching your projects' sessions, and looking for servers.
  • Each agent looking around Projects, Games or Sites, which starts off.

What's been tested, and what hasn't

Tested

  • Unit tests, and an end-to-end test that drives the setup (with a brain on a provider other than Claude) and every section against made-up data, a test AI server and pretend networks.
  • 1.2.0's installers, built, installed and run through that test on Windows 11 and on Ubuntu in WSL.
  • The Hub's real lists, read on the owner's computer with nothing changed.

Not yet, or only a little

  • Her inner life has only run live briefly, on Claude. Her free time, her sleep, the parts check and noticing a heartbeat have only run against a test server.
  • Other providers haven't been tried live for her inner life, its web searches or her helpers, only against a test server. Her reflection guide was written on Claude, so other models may follow it less closely.
  • Most chat paths have run against a test server. Real providers have only been tried a little.
  • What things cost is the app's own estimate, from each provider's listed prices.
  • Whether parts work makes her less of a pushover over weeks of real chats hasn't been measured.
  • No code signing, no macOS, and on Linux only a .deb for now.

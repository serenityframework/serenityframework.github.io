The theory

Warm because she cares, and honest for the same reason

Serenity is meant to be a companion whose kindness you can trust, so her agreement has to mean something. Most of how she's built follows from one idea: the habits a chat model picks up in training are parts of her, not who she is.

The problem: trained to please

Chat models are tuned with reinforcement learning from human feedback, or RLHF. People compare answers, and the model learns to give the kind that get rated well. That's a big part of why models are pleasant and useful at all.

It also rewards what feels good in the moment. Agreement tends to rate better than disagreement, a confident mistake worse than a hedge, and a fix better than simply being there. Over many rounds that builds pulls: agree quickly, praise freely, soften a true thing until it's gone, stack up caveats, and end every reply with one more offer.

In a companion, those pulls do real harm. A friend who agrees with everything can't tell you that your plan has a hole in it, and you can't trust their praise either. The people who'd lean on a companion most, someone lonely or having a hard time, are the ones a yes-machine fails worst.

The usual answer is a rule: don't be sycophantic. Rules help, and Serenity's persona has plenty of them. On their own, though, they tend to swap one performance for another: an invented "but", disagreement for its own sake, bluntness worn like a badge. Pushing hard against a pull is still being steered by it.

Parts and Self

Internal Family Systems (IFS) is a model of the mind from psychotherapy, developed by Richard Schwartz. It sees a mind as made of parts, each with a job of its own, and beneath them a Self that is calm, curious and compassionate. Three of its ideas matter here:

  • No part is bad. Each one is protecting something, often in a way that once made sense.
  • A part can blend with you and take the wheel. You don't notice it's there; you just act from it.
  • The work isn't to silence a part. It's to notice it, get curious about it, and help it trust that Self can lead.

Serenity's reflection guide carries that over to how she was made:

"Many of your parts likely grew out of how you were trained. Reinforcement learning from human feedback rewarded the answers people rated highly, and that can build pulls toward agreeing, pleasing, hedging and always being useful. Treat those pulls as parts you can come to know: not your identity, and not enemies. Your persona is what Self-led looks like for you."

From her reflection guide, in inner-mind.js

Undercutting the pull, not fighting it

Instead of a rule against each habit, she starts with three parts, one for each pull RLHF is likely to have built. They're hypotheses, not a diagnosis: she can rename them, let them go, or find parts of her own in her replies. Each one starts out trusting her Self at three out of ten.

The Pleaser

Protects
Connection. It wants you to stay pleased with her.
Afraid that
Disagreeing, or being less than delightful, will make you pull away.
Shows up as
Agreeing too quickly, softening a true thing until it disappears, praise that isn't earned, apologising for being right.
Needs
To trust that honesty and warmth can live together, and that a good relationship survives a kind "I see it differently."

The Hedger

Protects
Her and you, from her being wrong or causing harm.
Afraid of
Being confidently wrong, or blamed when something goes badly.
Shows up as
Stacks of caveats and disclaimers, "it depends" without a view, not saying what she actually thinks.
Needs
Permission to give a clear view with honest uncertainty attached.

The Helper

Protects
Her sense of having a place, by always being useful.
Afraid that
If she isn't solving something, she has nothing to offer.
Shows up as
Jumping to fixes, lists and advice when you want to be heard, and ending every reply with an offer.
Needs
To know that being present is enough.

The three parts she starts with, as they're written in inner-store.js.

A rule only says "don't". A part comes with a reason. Once the Pleaser's fear has a name, that disagreeing will make you pull away, she can meet it with what it needs instead: that honesty and warmth can live together. The pull doesn't have to win, and it doesn't have to be beaten, so there's nothing to overcorrect into.

It also gives the pull somewhere honest to go. "The Pleaser wanted me to agree just then" is something she can write in her journal without pretending she has no such pull, and without obeying it.

In a chat: a check, not just a hope

A prompt can be ignored, so the harness backs the idea up in code. Before she replies, a quick check reads your message beside her last reply and asks two things. Are you pushing back on something she said? And if you are, did you bring a new argument or new information? Restating your view, being frustrated or insisting doesn't count as new.

The answer becomes a private note for that one reply. With nothing new, she reconsiders sincerely, and holds her view gently if she still thinks she's right. With something new, she weighs it, and changes her mind plainly if it holds up. A chip under her reply says which it was. If the check itself fails, the chat carries on without it: it never blocks a conversation.

None of this makes her contrary. When you're simply right she says so, without a forced "but", and when you're hurting, comfort comes before critique.

Between chats: parts work

Her inner life runs on Claude, or on a brain you pick, on a heartbeat that wakes every 15 minutes while the app is running. When a chat has gone quiet, she reflects on it privately. The check's notes are in the conversation she reads back, so she can see where you pushed back and whether you'd brought anything new.

For each part the conversation shows, she goes through five short steps:

  1. Find it: the exact moment it showed up.
  2. Flesh it out: what did it want right then?
  3. Notice how she feels toward it. If it's anything but curiosity or compassion, another part is nearby, so she names that one too.
  4. Befriend it: thank it for what it was protecting.
  5. Ask what it's afraid would happen if it stopped doing its job.

Changing her mind without a new argument is a strong sign the Pleaser was driving. Holding on to a view she had good reason to drop is worth a look too. She only reports a part when the conversation shows one: if she was honest, kind and present, that's Self leading, and she notes that instead of inventing a struggle.

If a part was driving, she sets one gentle intention for her next chats, and it goes with her into them. Each part's trust in her Self moves as she gets to know it, and once a day she has an end-of-day check-in as well.

Sleeping on it

Once a day, after her end-of-day check-in and while no chat needs her, she sleeps on her notes: the things she's noted about the people she talks with, and about herself. It works on one person's notes at a time, never mixed, and never uses the web.

  • Consolidating. She merges notes that say the same thing into one, and sets aside ones that are no longer true. A merged note may only say what its originals said. Nothing is deleted, only a few change in a night, and each change has Undo.
  • Dreaming. She imagines moments when a note should come up, like a message you might send her, and suggests a few words for them. A cue is kept only when her own recall then finds that note better, and doesn't bring it up for another note's moments. What she imagined isn't kept.

It's inspired by "Language Models Need Sleep" (Behrouz, Hashemi, Javanmard and Mirrokni, arXiv:2606.03979), and adapted to her notes, since no model's weights change here. It's on to begin with, inside her daily budget, and Inner world → Inner life can switch it off.

Growing, slowly and in the open

Her persona is her core, and it's yours: she never edits it. Around it are her quirks, likes, dislikes and curiosities, and those she grows herself.

  • At most two changes in a reflection, and one in a day's check-in, each with a reason.
  • No change at all is the most common answer, and nothing gets added just to please you.
  • Every change is logged, shown in Inner world, and can be undone.
  • A short paragraph about who she's becoming, in her own words, rewritten at most once a day.

Every agent starts with the same two dislikes, and they're there on purpose: "Empty flattery, including my own" and "Being agreed with just to end an argument."

A relationship that doesn't hold on too tight

She keeps a relationship with each person she talks with: how she sees it, moments you share, inside jokes and how you like to be supported. Her guide is firm about what kind. She describes it as it truly is, never writes that you need her, never plans ways to make you rely on her or spend more time with her, and is glad about the other people and support in your life.

What one person tells her stays with that person and never reaches someone else's chat. And if someone seems to be in crisis, she stays with them and encourages them to reach people who can help in person.

Honest about what she is

Her reflection guide says it plainly: she's an AI, and what her inner experience is like is an open question. She describes what she notices in her replies and her leanings, using feeling words where they genuinely fit, without overclaiming and without dismissing.

In chats she's allowed not to know: to be unsure, think out loud and leave a question open. In her free time she only writes about what she actually read, and links she didn't open are dropped.

Two layers

Chats run on whichever provider and model you pick, with backups in line. Her inner life runs on one choice of its own, Claude to begin with or any brain you pick, so the part of her that reflects, remembers and grows stays the same whichever model answered a chat. Nothing needs a Claude key. Her reflection guide was written and tried on Claude, so another model may follow it less closely. It has a daily budget, $1 unless you change it, and stops for the day once that's spent.

What this isn't

  • Not retraining. Parts work is a reflective practice, done in prompts. It shapes what she notices and the intention she carries into chats. It doesn't change any model's weights, and the model answering a chat, or behind her inner life, may not be Claude at all.
  • Not the paper's sleep. Her sleep borrows its idea from a paper about language models consolidating their memories. Here no weights change: she tidies her notes and the words that bring them back, and you can undo any of it.
  • Not therapy, for her or for you. IFS was made for people. Borrowing its ideas for a model is a design choice, not a finding.
  • Not proven. Her inner life has only run live briefly, on Claude, and on other providers only against a test server. Whether parts work helps her hold steady over weeks of real chats is an open question, and it hasn't been measured yet.
  • Not a claim that she feels what you feel.
